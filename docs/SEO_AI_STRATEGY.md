# SEO & AI Strategy

This document governs how the Kodu marketing site earns discovery — by people searching, and by AI systems that read, summarize, and cite the web. The strategy is built on the same foundation as the rest of the ecosystem: be genuinely useful, be accurate, and never trade trust for traffic.

The single rule that overrides every tactic here: **we optimize by being the clearest, most trustworthy true answer to a real homeowner question.** Tactics that conflict with that rule are not used, even if they would rank.

## Why both search and AI matter

Homeowners increasingly find answers two ways: traditional search results, and AI assistants that read pages and answer directly. Kodu wants to be the source those systems trust and quote. That requires content that is unambiguous, well-structured, factually grounded, and quotable in small, self-contained pieces.

## Foundations (do these for every page)

- **One real question per page.** This is also the content rule (`CONTENT_STRATEGY.md`). A page that answers one clear question is easy for search engines and AI systems to match and cite.
- **Answer early.** State the direct answer near the top in plain language, then expand. Both readers and AI extractors reward a clear lead answer.
- **Descriptive, durable titles and headings.** Headings phrased as the homeowner's question or its answer. Stable, descriptive URLs (see `INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md`).
- **Self-contained sections.** Each section should make sense if quoted on its own. AI systems lift sections; ambiguous fragments get misquoted or skipped.
- **Accurate metadata.** Title and description that truthfully summarize the page. No clickbait, no mismatch between promise and content.
- **Clean semantic HTML.** Correct heading order and landmarks help both crawlers and assistive tech (see `ACCESSIBILITY.md`). Good structure is good SEO and good accessibility at once.

## Connected journey strategy

Kodu is not building isolated SEO articles. Search and AI discovery should bring
homeowners into a connected learning path about The Private Homeowner Property Record.

Every page answers one real homeowner question, then points to the next
chapter in the learning journey.

Every page should have:

- One primary homeowner question.
- One direct answer near the top.
- One clear next chapter.
- One restrained Kodu connection appropriate to the reader's stage.

Do not use every page to explain the full Kodu product. A page about why home
history gets lost should not also try to explain every part of the app. It
should answer the question, define the problem clearly, and point to the next
chapter where the reader is ready for the solution.

The foundation sequence is the canonical learning path:

1. What Is a Home Property Record?
2. Every Home Has a Story
3. Why Homeowners Lose Their Property History
4. How Kodu Creates Your Property's Memory
5. Inside a Kodu Property Record

Search snippets and AI summaries should be able to understand where a page sits
in that path. Use breadcrumb text, related-guide cards, and progression CTAs to
make the sequence clear.

## Answer boundaries

A page should be quotable for its own question, not for every Kodu concept.

For each page:

- Define the central term or answer once, early.
- Keep supporting sections focused on that answer.
- Link to the next page for the next question.
- Avoid repeating the same product explanation across the ecosystem.
- Use request-access language only after the educational answer has done its
  work.

This helps search engines and AI systems classify Kodu correctly while reducing
duplicate, drifting explanations.

## Structured data

- Use schema.org structured data where it genuinely describes the content: articles/guides, FAQ where a page truly answers discrete questions, organization, and breadcrumb.
- Structured data must match the visible page exactly. Marking up content that is not present, or questions that are not really answered, is misrepresentation and is not allowed.
- Do not add FAQ markup just to win result real estate; only mark up real, useful Q&A.

## Topical authority

- Build depth around the property record domain rather than chasing unrelated high-traffic terms.
- Cover the full property boundary (land, site, exterior, systems, projects, documents) so the site reads as a coherent authority on documenting a home, not a scatter of disconnected posts.
- Interlink within topics using the canonical-page model so authority concentrates on the right pages.
- Build future libraries around Property Records, Maintenance, Systems,
  Projects, Insurance, Buying & Selling, Homeowner Knowledge, and Kodu Education.
  Each library should connect back to the foundation sequence when the reader
  needs the category explained.
- For system pages, cover how the system works, what homeowners should know,
  what records matter, what proof should be saved, and what future owners
  should inherit.

## Being quotable by AI systems

- Lead with a crisp, correct, standalone answer.
- Define terms in homeowner language where they first appear.
- Prefer plain declarative sentences over clever phrasing for the core answer; save voice for the surrounding explanation.
- Keep claims verifiable and specific. Vague or hedged statements get dropped; false ones damage trust if surfaced.
- Make the brand identity unambiguous on every page: Kodu is a private homeowner property record platform. This prevents AI systems from miscategorizing Kodu as public-records search, storage, a CRM, or a marketplace.

## Hard limits — what we never do

- No keyword stuffing, doorway pages, or thin pages written only to rank.
- No fear-based or hype-based copy to manufacture clicks. This conflicts directly with the trust-and-tone rules.
- No unsupported claims (savings, insurance outcomes, home-value impact) for the sake of a query.
- No structured data that does not match the page.
- No cloaking, hidden text, or content shown to crawlers but not users.
- No manufactured urgency in titles or descriptions.
- No content positioning Kodu as something it is not (public search portal, marketplace, generic storage).

## Measuring success honestly

Success is not raw traffic. It is the right homeowners finding accurate answers and trusting the source. We watch whether pages answer their question well (engagement, low pogo-sticking), whether AI systems represent Kodu correctly, and whether discovery growth comes from useful evergreen content rather than churned, disposable pages.

When an SEO tactic and a trust principle conflict, the trust principle wins. Always.
