# Writing Style Guide

This document governs how Kodu sounds in writing across the marketing ecosystem. The voice is part of the product. If a page is accurate but sounds alarmed, salesy, or technical, it has failed.

## Voice in one line

Calm, clear, practical, and respectful — a knowledgeable friend who helps a homeowner understand and document their property, never a salesperson and never an alarm.

## Core voice traits

- **Honest.** We say what is true and only what is true. We do not imply outcomes we cannot support.
- **Calm.** No urgency, no pressure, no exclamation-driven energy.
- **Clear.** Plain language a homeowner understands on the first read.
- **Practical.** Every explanation points toward something the reader can actually understand or do.
- **Respectful.** We assume the reader is intelligent and capable. We do not condescend or over-explain the obvious.
- **Confidence-building.** The reader should feel more in control after reading, not more anxious.

## Homeowner language, not contractor jargon

Kodu's product structure may be informed by contractor and industry logic, but the writing stays in homeowner language. Translate industry terms into everyday words. When a technical term is genuinely useful, define it in plain language the first time it appears, then use it.

- Prefer "the record of your home" over "asset documentation."
- Prefer "warranty" over "manufacturer coverage instrument."
- Prefer "who can see your records" over "RBAC / permission scopes."

## Tone by context

- **Guides and concepts:** warm, instructive, unhurried.
- **Product pages:** straightforward and concrete; describe what Kodu does without superlatives.
- **Pricing:** plain and transparent. State the trial and terms simply. No dark patterns, no pressure.
- **Privacy and trust:** reassuring through clarity, not promises. Explain how it works.
- **Any maintenance or guidance content:** supportive, never alarmist. "Here's what's worth keeping track of," never "you're at risk if you don't."

## Words and phrasing to avoid

Avoid hype and pressure:
- "revolutionary," "game-changing," "the only," "best-in-class," "unlock," "supercharge," "effortlessly"
- manufactured urgency: "act now," "before it's too late," "don't wait," countdown language

Avoid fear:
- "at risk," "disaster," "nightmare," "lose everything," "protect yourself before…"

Avoid vague salesmanship and unsupported claims:
- promises about money saved, insurance results, or home value unless they are specific and supportable (and most are not — so they stay out)

Avoid jargon dropped without explanation:
- contractor, legal, or developer terms presented as if the reader should already know them

## Grammar and mechanics

- **Sentences:** short to medium. One idea per sentence. Break up anything that needs to be reread.
- **Voice:** active. "Kodu keeps your records private," not "records are kept private."
- **Person:** address the reader as "you." Refer to the product as "Kodu."
- **Numbers:** spell out one through nine in prose; numerals for 10+ and for all measurements, prices, and counts.
- **Capitalization:** sentence case for headings and buttons. Capitalize "Kodu" and product-feature proper nouns only where they are genuinely proper names.
- **Punctuation:** minimal exclamation points (effectively none in body copy). Oxford comma. Em dashes used sparingly.
- **Lists:** use them when they genuinely aid scanning, not to pad. Keep list items parallel in structure.

## Headings

- Phrase headings as the homeowner's question or its plain answer.
- Make each heading meaningful on its own — someone scanning headings should understand the page.
- Avoid clever-but-empty headings that sound nice and say nothing.

## Claims and accuracy

- Every product claim must be true of the actual product today. Aspirational features are not described as if they exist.
- Comparisons describe differences honestly; they do not disparage other tools or categories.
- If a statement cannot be supported, it is removed, not softened with weasel words.

## Examples

Off-voice:
> Don't let your home's history slip away! Kodu's revolutionary platform protects you from costly disasters and unlocks total peace of mind — sign up before it's too late.

On-voice:
> Your home accumulates a history — repairs, warranties, projects, and the proof behind them. Kodu keeps that history in one private place, so it's there when you need it.

Off-voice:
> Leverage best-in-class asset documentation to maximize property value.

On-voice:
> A well-kept record is something you can hand to the next owner. Kodu is built to keep that record intact from the start.

## Final check

Before publishing, read the page aloud. If any sentence sounds anxious, pushy, or like a pitch, rewrite it until it sounds like calm, honest help.
