What is a home property record?

A home property record is the private, organized history of your home: documents, warranties, repairs, photos, projects, and proof that help you understand what happened, what matters, and what is next.

Every home carries a story, from the day you moved in to every repair, upgrade, maintenance decision, and document created along the way.

A property record helps you keep that story in one private place so it is easy to understand, easy to manage, and not lost across email, drawers, phone photos, and memory.

01What belongs

What belongs in a home property record?

If it adds context, proves work, protects coverage, or tells part of the story, include it.

Documents

Deeds, disclosures, permits, inspections, and important letters.

Warranties

Appliance, HVAC, roofing, windows, fixtures, and more.

Receipts

Proof of purchase, installation, materials, and service work.

Repairs

Work done, dates, cost, who did it, and notes from the visit.

Photos

Before, after, project, issue, upgrade, and milestone photos.

Projects

Plans, budgets, material specs, decisions, and timelines.

Manuals

How-to guides, care instructions, and spec sheets.

Communications

Emails, quotes, explanations, and important notes.

Proof

Appraisals, improvements, and records that support what changed.

When a property record matters most

  • When you need to make a repair or maintenance decision
  • When you are planning a project or upgrade
  • When it is time to sell or transfer your home
  • When you need proof for a claim or question

Why privacy and control matter

Your home record can include sensitive details about your home, your finances, and your life. It should stay private by default, and you should decide who can see it.

03FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is a home property record the same as a home inventory?

No. A home inventory usually lists belongings. A home property record focuses on the property itself: systems, repairs, warranties, documents, projects, photos, and proof.

What if I do not have all my records yet?

Start with what you have. Add warranties, receipts, photos, manuals, permits, and repair notes over time. A useful property record can grow gradually.

Who should have access to my property record?

That should be your choice. You may want a spouse, family member, or trusted helper to see parts of the record, but it should stay private by default.

Will this help when I sell my home?

A well-kept record can help explain the care, repairs, and improvements that shaped the property. It can also give the next owner a clearer starting point.

Is Kodu only for homeowners?

Kodu is written for people who own and care for a home, including new owners, long-term owners, inherited-home owners, and trusted household members who help manage the property.

Is my data secure and private?

Kodu is built around private homeowner control. The page does not make a blanket security guarantee, but the product direction is clear: the record is private by default and shared intentionally.
