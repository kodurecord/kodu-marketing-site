Property Memory

How Kodu creates your property’s memory

Kodu gives the story of a home a private structure. The blueprint organizes the property, the vault stores the proof, the timeline shows what happened, and guidance helps homeowners understand what matters next.

One private structure, built around the home.

This is the first chapter where Kodu becomes the answer. The point is not more places to put files. The point is a record that remembers the property in a way a homeowner can understand and control.

01Record anatomy

Kodu treats property memory as layers of one record.

Each layer has a different job, but none of them lives alone. The structure works because every detail stays connected to the property.

BlueprintOrganizes the property

The blueprint gives every part of the home a place.

Instead of treating the home like a loose folder, Kodu starts with the structure of the property itself. Rooms, systems, appliances, projects, documents, and photos can all connect back to the part of the home they explain.

  • Home details stay attached to the property, not a random file name.
  • Records can be understood by location, system, project, or time.
  • The homeowner gets a calmer way to see what the home contains.
VaultStores the proof

The vault gives proof a place to live.

Receipts, warranties, photos, service notes, and documents become more useful when they are not separated from the property. The vault keeps proof close to the home detail, project, or timeline event it supports.

  • Proof is easier to find when a decision or question comes up.
  • Photos and documents keep their relationship to the home.
  • The record can explain what happened without relying on memory alone.
TimelineTells the story

The timeline shows how the home changed.

A property memory is not just a collection. It has sequence. The timeline helps a homeowner see repairs, replacements, projects, and updates as a story that unfolds over time.

  • Work no longer feels like disconnected moments.
  • Dates, reasons, photos, and proof can travel together.
  • A future handoff begins with context instead of guesswork.
GuidanceExplains what matters

Guidance turns stored information into homeowner context.

Kodu helps the record feel understandable. Guidance can point out what may need attention, what proof is helpful to keep, and what a homeowner may want to remember before the next repair, project, or transfer.

  • The record becomes easier to act on.
  • Homeowners can see gaps without feeling blamed.
  • Useful next steps stay tied to the property itself.
ControlHomeowner approval

The homeowner stays in charge of the record.

Kodu's contribution model is designed around permission. The Kodu Property Code identifies the property record, a Kodu Property Tag can point trusted people to the Kodu Gateway, and the homeowner reviews what becomes part of the permanent private memory.

  • A marker can open a contribution path without exposing the home record.
  • New information waits for homeowner review before it belongs.
  • The record grows through controlled additions, not open access.
02Blueprint layer

The blueprint organizes the property.

Kodu begins by giving the home a shape. The record can hold systems, spaces, appliances, projects, and details because it knows they belong to one property.

This helps a homeowner stop asking, "Where did I put that?" and start asking, "Which part of the home does this explain?"

  • 1
    Home details are connected to rooms, systems, and projects.
  • 2
    The record feels like a map of the property, not a pile of uploads.
  • 3
    Future updates have a natural place to land.
03Vault layer

The vault stores the proof.

The vault gives proof a place to live, so receipts, warranties, photos, and service records are not separated from the property.

A document becomes more useful when it stays connected to the repair, system, project, or date it explains. Kodu is designed to preserve that relationship.

  • 1
    Proof stays close to the detail it supports.
  • 2
    Documents and photos are easier to understand later.
  • 3
    Warranty, service, and project questions can start with context.
04Timeline layer

The timeline tells the story.

The timeline lets the home remember what happened in order: repairs, replacements, inspections, projects, and proof that belongs to each moment.

A homeowner does not need a perfect history for the timeline to help. Even a few connected events can make future decisions clearer.

  • 1
    Repairs and projects become part of a continuous property story.
  • 2
    Photos, notes, and proof can travel with the event they explain.
  • 3
    A future owner can understand the care behind the home.
05Guidance layer

Guidance explains what matters.

Kodu is not just a place where information waits. It helps the homeowner understand which details may matter for care, planning, proof, and future handoff.

Guidance should feel calm, not urgent. It gives the record a voice that helps a homeowner make sense of what is already there and what may be worth adding.

  • 1
    Helpful context appears beside the property detail it belongs to.
  • 2
    Gaps become easier to see without turning the record into a chore.
  • 3
    The homeowner can move from stored proof to a clearer next step.
06Privacy and control layer

The homeowner stays in control.

Kodu's access model is built so the property can receive helpful contributions without giving up homeowner approval.

A Kodu Property Code is the property’s digital identity. A Kodu Property Tag is a physical access marker. The Kodu Gateway is the controlled contribution path. Review is the homeowner approval layer. The Record is the permanent private memory.

  • 1
    Trusted people can be pointed to the right contribution path.
  • 2
    The homeowner decides what is accepted into the record.
  • 3
    The property memory stays private, structured, and continuous.
