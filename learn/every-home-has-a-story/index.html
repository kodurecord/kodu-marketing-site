Property memory

Every home has a story.

A home is shaped by every repair, improvement, warranty, inspection, project, and decision made over time. The story already exists. The question is whether it will be remembered.

See why homeowners lose that story Request access

Short answer

A home's story is the record of what changed, who worked on it, what was repaired or improved, what proof exists, and what future owners should understand.

It is not only a folder of documents. It is the memory of the property, built from ordinary moments that become useful later.

What changed

Repairs, replacements, improvements, inspections, and updates all become part of the property.

Who helped

Service providers, inspectors, installers, and household decisions add context to the work.

What proof exists

Receipts, warranties, photos, notes, and documents explain what happened when memory fades.

01Living history

Your home changes over time

A home is not frozen on the day you move in. It is shaped by maintenance, seasons, choices, materials, repairs, inspections, and the people who care for it.

Some changes are easy to notice, like a roof replacement or finished basement. Others are quieter: a new shutoff valve, a corrected drainage issue, a service note about an aging appliance. Together, those details explain the home better than any single document can.

02Everyday proof

Small moments become the story

The most useful history often starts with ordinary homeowner moments. A photo, a receipt, or a short note can explain a decision years later.

The service visit

A technician explains what was repaired, what may need attention later, and which part was replaced.

The project decision

A material choice, permit, estimate, and before photo become part of the reason the project makes sense.

The photo you almost skip

A quick photo can show condition, location, and timing in a way a memory often cannot.

04Memory fades

Why the story gets harder to remember

The work is real, but the details are usually scattered across the places life already happens: email, paper, photos, text messages, invoices, and memory.

This is normal. Home information does not arrive in one neat format. It arrives when something breaks, when a project starts, when a warranty card shows up, or when a photo is taken quickly before the wall closes.

Email Estimates, invoices, inspection notes, and warranty PDFs
Phone Photos, screenshots, texts, and quick reminders
Paper Receipts, manuals, permits, and handwritten service notes
Memory Why a choice was made, who did the work, and what changed

What future homeowners should inherit

A future owner should not have to start from zero. The record can explain what changed, what proof exists, and what still deserves attention.

06FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What does it mean that a home has a story?

A home's story is the record of what changed, who worked on it, what was repaired or improved, what proof exists, and what future owners should understand.

What kinds of moments become part of a home's history?

Repairs, service visits, inspections, warranties, photos, receipts, projects, material choices, and homeowner decisions can all become part of the home's history.

Do small repairs really matter later?

They can. A small repair may explain a recurring issue, confirm which part was replaced, show who did the work, or help the next person understand the system faster.

Should I document my home even if I am not planning to sell?

Yes. The story helps you first. It can support maintenance decisions, future projects, warranty questions, and a clearer handoff later if the home changes owners.

How does this connect to Kodu?

Kodu is built to help a homeowner preserve this story privately, with records, proof, projects, photos, systems, and timeline events organized around the property.
